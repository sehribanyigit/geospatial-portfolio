Antarctica · MSc Research · 2023

Renewable Energy Potential in Antarctica

A spatial assessment of renewable-energy potential across Antarctic research stations using long-term climate and reanalysis data.

The study compared 20 research stations and included a preliminary analysis for the Turkish Research Base on Horseshoe Island. It brought together long-term MERRA-2 data, station-scale comparison and spatial reasoning to examine place-specific renewable-energy conditions.

Published as peer-reviewed research, the work forms an early link between my academic background and my current focus on applied geospatial analysis, Earth observation and GeoAI.

About

Curiosity starts with a place.

I’m Şehriban, a geomatics engineer exploring spatial analysis, Earth observation and GeoAI.

My path began in research, studying renewable energy potential in Antarctica. Since then, I’ve become increasingly interested in what spatial data can reveal beyond a location on a map: patterns, relationships and questions I might not have thought to ask before.

I build projects to follow that curiosity. Whether I’m analysing urban environments or evaluating a GeoAI model, I want to understand what the data shows, where its limits are and how to share the findings clearly.

Working with maps has changed how I see the world. I use spatial data to explore how places and people relate, question what a map can—and cannot—tell us, and follow the new questions that emerge.
