Selected work / Earth observation / Adelaide · 2026

Adelaide Park Lands Cooling

Does land surface temperature change consistently with distance from the Park Lands?

Landsat · LST · NDVI · 100 m grid

Explore the repository ↗

The finding

There was no single cooling distance.

Park interiors appeared cooler in the primary scene, but temperatures outside the boundary varied across locations and distance bands. The observations did not support a uniform cooling-reach threshold.

The result describes a spatial pattern in land surface temperature, not a measured effect on air temperature or human thermal comfort.

01 / Study area

A thermal view of the Park Lands.

The map shows the spatial variation in daytime land surface temperature used to frame the distance analysis.

02 / Approach

From satellite pixels to spatial comparisons.

I prepared a cloud-masked Landsat 9 scene from 26 January 2026, derived land surface temperature and NDVI, and aligned the rasters to the study area.

A 100 m analysis grid provided 5,867 valid cells. Signed distance from the Park Lands boundary distinguished interior and exterior locations; I compared temperature distributions across distance bands and sections of the surrounding city.

An independent Landsat 8 scene from 3 February 2026 provided a sensitivity check of the main pattern.

Landsat scenes → LST and NDVI → 100 m grid → Boundary distance → Sensitivity check

03 / What the analysis showed

The pattern was spatially uneven.

01

Cooler interiors

Park Lands cells were generally cooler in the primary daytime scene, although values varied within the park.

02

No clean distance gradient

Outside temperature did not increase steadily with distance in a way that justified one universal reach.

03

Context matters

Vegetation and the surrounding built landscape are part of the observed variation; distance alone did not explain it.

04 / Interpretation

What this study can—and cannot—claim.

This is an observational comparison of daytime surface temperatures from particular satellite scenes. Land surface temperature is different from air temperature and does not directly measure thermal comfort.

The analysis identifies heterogeneous associations around the Park Lands. It does not establish that the park caused lower temperatures at a specific distance, or that the same pattern holds on every day.
